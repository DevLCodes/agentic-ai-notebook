Confidence Scoring

A confidence score is a decision input: answer, hedge, hand off to a human, or try a bigger model. It is only useful if the number is calibrated and something happens because of it.

Signals

No single signal is enough. Each measures something different, and each can be fooled.

Combining signals

p(correct) = σ(b₀ + b₁·rerank + b₂·margin + b₃·agreement + b₄·groundedness + …)

A hand-weighted average is the usual first version. It’s fine as a start, but its output is not a probability and the weights are guesses. Once you have a few hundred labelled outcomes, fit a logistic regression: a handful of coefficients, trained in milliseconds, whose sizes show which signals actually predict correctness. Hard rules run first: a calculator mismatch or a failed guardrail escalates whatever the model says.

Self-consistency

Sample the same question N times (often 5) at a temperature above zero. If the model knows the answer, the samples agree; if it is guessing, they scatter.

semantic entropy = −Σ p(cluster) · log₂ p(cluster) 0 = all agree · log₂ 5 ≈ 2.32 = all differ

Group the samples by meaning, not by string: “29.8%” and “about 30% (29.8%)” are the same answer. Entropy over those meaning clusters is semantic entropy (Farquhar et al., Nature, 2024). The cost is N model calls per question, so keep it for expensive mistakes or a sample.

Calibration

A cosine of 0.83 or a rerank logit of 7.2 orders results; neither means “83% likely to be right”. Calibrated means that of all answers scored 0.7, about 70% are correct.

ECE = Σᵢ (nᵢ / N) · |accuracyᵢ − confidenceᵢ| over score bins i
MethodHowWhen
Temperature scalingDivide the logit by one number TKeeps ranking; can only squeeze or stretch around 50%
Platt scalingp = σ(a·logit(s) + b), a one-feature logistic regressionThe bias b can shift scores down, which overconfident systems need
Isotonic regressionA monotone step functionMost flexible; needs roughly a thousand labels or more, overfits small sets

Fit all three on a held-out labelled set, never on the data you report, and re-fit after any model or index change.

The threshold is a cost decision

This is selective prediction: you trade coverage for accuracy. The right point depends on what a wrong answer costs compared with a person’s time. A wrong revenue figure in an analyst’s model costs far more than a wrong page number.

best threshold = argmin over t of cost_wrong × wrong_answered(t) + cost_escalation × escalated(t)
  • A hedge band between “answer” and “escalate”, where the answer ships with its caveat and sources.
  • Per-segment thresholds: numeric questions, summaries and out-of-scope requests each get their own, chosen on their own labelled slice.
  • Hard rules beat scores: a calculator mismatch escalates whatever the blend says.

Try it: pick the threshold

Twenty labelled answers with calibrated confidence. Answer when the score is at or above the threshold; otherwise send the question to an analyst.

In production

Track the escalation rate per day and per segment. A sudden drop after a new embedding model, reranker or index usually means the score distribution moved, not that the system got better. Re-fit calibration on the labelled set after every model or index change, and feed analyst decisions from the queue back in as new labels (see evals and testing and rollout).

  • Don’t show a cosine score as a percentage: it is not a probability.
  • Don’t trust logprobs as truth: they score the wording.
  • Don’t use “how sure are you?” on its own.
  • Don’t set one global threshold once and never revisit it.

Saying it in an interview

“Confidence exists to route each answer: answer, hedge, escalate or retry with a bigger model. I’d combine retrieval signals (top rerank score, margin, source agreement) with groundedness from the citation checks in a logistic regression fitted on a few hundred labelled outcomes, calibrate it with Platt scaling on a held-out set and report ECE. Thresholds come from the cost of a wrong answer versus an escalation, per segment, with hard rules like a calculator mismatch overriding the score. In production I watch escalation rate per segment and re-fit after any model change.”
