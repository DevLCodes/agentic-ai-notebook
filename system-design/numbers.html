Numbers cheat sheet: tokens, cost and latency

Back-of-the-envelope numbers for AI system design: how many tokens a page, file, prompt or chat turn is, input and output prices by model tier, monthly cost estimates, classifier vs LLM cost, and latency.

Memorise these first

  1. 1 token ≈ 4 characters ≈ ¾ of an English word. 1,000 words ≈ 1,300 tokens; one page ≈ 600 tokens.
  2. Output costs 4 to 8 times more than input per token, so long answers are expensive and short labels are cheap.
  3. Three price tiers per 1M tokens (input / output): small about $0.10 / $0.40, mid about $0.30 / $2.50, large about $1.25 / $10.
  4. A chat turn is about 5K tokens in and 250 out. The prompt is mostly system prompt, history and retrieved chunks, not the question.
  5. An agent task is 30K to 100K tokens in, because every step re-sends the context.
  6. Cost = input tokens × input price + output tokens × output price. Per day × 30 = per month.
  7. A classifier is 10 to 100 times cheaper and faster than an LLM on high-volume fixed-label work: a few dollars vs $100+ per million items, 10 ms vs 1 s.
  8. Batch is about half price; cached input is 75 to 90% off.

Prices are illustrative list prices for Flash-Lite, Flash and Pro class models at the time of writing. They change often, so say "roughly" in an interview and show the method, not the exact number.

Worked monthly estimates

One-time indexing: 1M pages × 600 tokens = 600M tokens. Embedding them costs about $10 to $90. Adding an LLM-written context line per chunk with a mid model costs a few hundred dollars more.

Do not forget labelling: 5K to 10K human labels at $0.10 to $1 each is $500 to $10K one time, or have an LLM label them and experts check a sample. The classifier still wins when volume is high, labels are fixed and the data is labelled.

Cutting the bill, biggest lever first

  1. Use a smaller model where evals allow (routing, cascade): often 5 to 10 × cheaper.
  2. Send fewer input tokens: RAG instead of whole documents, top 3 to 5 chunks, summarise old turns, load only the tools needed.
  3. Prompt caching for the fixed system prompt, tools and shared documents: 75 to 90% off those tokens.
  4. Cap output: short answers, JSON labels instead of explanations.
  5. Batch mode for anything that can wait: about 50% off.
  6. Response or semantic cache for repeated questions (check correctness and per-user data).
  7. Replace the LLM with a classifier on high-volume, fixed-label paths.

Saying it in an interview

"Let me estimate. 50K conversations a day, about 6 turns, and each turn sends roughly 5K tokens: 2K system prompt, 2K of retrieved chunks and some history, with about 250 tokens out. That's 1.5 billion input and 75 million output tokens a day. On a Flash-class model at roughly 30 cents in and $2.50 out per million, that's about $640 a day, or around $19K a month; on a Pro-class model, about four times that. To cut it, I'd cache the system prompt, route easy turns to a smaller model, and keep only the top chunks. If part of the traffic is fixed-label tagging at high volume, a small fine-tuned classifier does it for a few percent of the LLM cost."
