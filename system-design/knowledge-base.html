What a knowledge base is

In a retrieval system, the knowledge base is everything the assistant is allowed to search: the source documents, the cleaned and chunked copies, their metadata, and the indexes built over them. It is the system of record for "what the assistant knows" outside the model's weights.

Think of it as a data pipeline with a search engine at the end. Every layer is rebuildable from the layer before it. Keeping the raw copies means you can change parsing or chunking without re-downloading 10 million files through rate-limited APIs.

Knowledge base, fine-tuning or memory

OptionUse it forNot for
Knowledge base (RAG)Facts that change, need citations, or have access rulesTeaching a style or a new skill
Fine-tuningFormat, tone, domain vocabulary, a narrow task done many timesFresh facts, per-user permissions, anything needing a source link
Agent memoryWhat one user said or prefers across sessionsShared company knowledge

Most enterprise assistants are knowledge-base first. Fine-tuning a model on company documents bakes in facts that go stale and ignores who is allowed to see what.

Parsing and cleaning

  • Extract structure, not just text. Keep headings, lists and tables. They drive chunking and give each chunk its heading path.
  • Handle each format. PDFs need layout-aware extraction, scanned files need OCR, slides read best one slide at a time, spreadsheets as rows with their header.
  • Strip boilerplate. Navigation menus, footers and "last edited by" lines add noise and match every query.
  • Deduplicate. Hash normalised content. Copies of the same policy in five folders should become one document with five locations, or the top results fill with duplicates.

Chunking

Models and search work on passages, not whole documents, so each document is split into chunks. Chunks that are too small lose context; chunks that are too large dilute the match and waste the prompt.

StrategyHowGood for
Fixed sizeEvery N tokens with overlapPlain text with no structure; a quick baseline
RecursiveSplit on sections, then paragraphs, then sentences until under the size limitThe sensible default for mixed content
By headingOne chunk per section, split further only if too longWikis, policies, runbooks
SemanticEmbed sentences and cut where meaning shiftsLong unstructured text such as transcripts

A sane default is 300 to 800 tokens with 10 to 20% overlap. Always prefix each chunk with its document title and heading path, such as "Leave policy > India > Parental leave", so it stands alone. Strategies, overlap and a hands-on lab: Chunking.

Metadata on every chunk

Metadata is what lets you filter, cite, refresh and delete. At minimum store: doc_id, chunk_id, version, source and URL, title and heading path, updated_at, author, space or project, document type, region or language, the access control list as allowed_principals, and the embedding_model_version.

Keeping it fresh

A knowledge base is only as good as its last sync. Combine push (webhooks) for speed with pull (a periodic reconciliation crawl) for correctness, process each changed document as a unit, and delete chunks from old versions. The initial load and full rebuilds are covered in Backfills and re-indexing.

Track three health numbers: ingestion lag (edit to searchable), documents failing to parse, and coverage (documents in the source vs documents in the catalog).
