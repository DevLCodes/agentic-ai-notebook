Reranking

A second, more careful pass that reorders retrieved chunks so the best ones reach the prompt.

  3. Where it sits

Why rerank

Retrieval is tuned for recall: it casts a wide net quickly. Reranking is tuned for precision: it reads each candidate carefully and puts the best few on top.

The prompt only holds a handful of chunks, and models pay most attention to the first ones. Getting the right chunk into position 1 instead of position 7 is often the cheapest quality win in a RAG system.

How rerankers work

A retrieval model (bi-encoder) embeds the query and each chunk separately, which is why chunk vectors can be precomputed. A cross-encoder takes the query and one chunk as a single input and outputs a relevance score. It is far more accurate but must run once per candidate at query time, so you only use it on the top 50 or so.

Where it sits

After reranking, apply cheap business rules: boost recent and authoritative sources, demote archived ones, and collapse several chunks from one document so the top results are not all copies of the same page.

Options

OptionNotes
Managed ranking APIsVertex AI ranking API, Cohere Rerank and similar; no model hosting
Open cross-encodersBGE reranker, ms-marco MiniLM family; host on GPU or CPU for small batches
LLM as rerankerAsk a model to score or order candidates; flexible and accurate but slower and costlier
Late interaction (ColBERT)Token-level matching; between bi- and cross-encoders in cost and quality

When to skip it

  • Tiny corpora where the top 5 are already right on your golden set.
  • Strict latency budgets where 200 ms matters more than the quality gain; measure first.
  • Exact lookups (an ID search) where there is only one right answer.

Measure with MRR and recall@5 on your golden set, with and without the reranker. Keep it only if the numbers move.
