The whole path in one picture

Retrieval gives you candidate evidence. Everything after it decides whether the user gets a correct, sourced answer or a confident wrong one. Say these seven steps in order: Rank, Pack, Prompt, Generate, Check, Ship, Log.

The seven steps

StepWhat happensWhy it matters
1 Rank and gateA cross-encoder reranks the top 20 and keeps the best 5. If the best score is under a threshold, do not answer: say not found, ask a clarifying question, or widen the search.Stops the model guessing from weak evidence. The gate is in code, not the prompt.
2 PackRemove duplicates, fit a token budget (about 4K), put the strongest chunk first, keep id, source and date with each chunk.Models attend less to the middle of long contexts; ids make citations checkable.
3 PromptInstructions first, then chunks wrapped as data (<doc id="c12" source="…" date="…">), then the question. Rules: answer only from the docs, cite ids, say not found if missing.Wrapping retrieved text as data is also the defence against indirect prompt injection.
4 GenerateStructured output such as {answer, citations: [ids], found}, streamed to the user.Code can read the citations; streaming gives first words in about a second.
5 CheckIn code: every cited id is one you retrieved and the user can see; every number appears in a chunk or tool result; scan for personal data and unsafe content. On failure, regenerate once, then fall back.Catches hallucinated sources and numbers before anyone sees them.
6 ShipAnswer with clickable sources and thumbs up or down. Low confidence or a sensitive topic goes to a human.Users can verify; feedback becomes data.
7 LogOne trace: query, chunk ids and versions, scores, prompt version, model, latency, cost, feedback.Lets you debug any bad answer, and thumbs-down cases grow the golden set.

Bi-encoder vs cross-encoder (step 1 in detail)

Bi-encoder (embeddings)Cross-encoder (reranker)
InputQuestion and chunk encoded separately into vectors, compared with cosine similarityQuestion and chunk fed in together as one input; outputs one relevance score
PrecomputeYes: chunk vectors are made once at indexingNo: must run once per (question, chunk) pair at query time
SpeedMillions of chunks in milliseconds (with an ANN index)About 20 pairs in roughly 50 to 150 ms
AccuracyApproximate: question and chunk never see each otherHigher: catches chunks that are on topic but don't answer the question
JobRetrieval: millions → top 20 (with keyword search)Reranking: top 20 → best 5
ExamplesGemini / OpenAI / Cohere embeddings, BGE, E5Cohere Rerank, Vertex AI ranking API, BGE-reranker, ms-marco cross-encoders

Example. Question: "Can I get a refund after 30 days?" Embedding search may rank a chunk about a "30-day free trial" highly because the words and topic are close. The cross-encoder reads both together, sees it does not answer a refund question, scores it about 0.1, and lifts the refund-policy chunk to about 0.9.

Its score is also what the step 1 gate uses: if even the best reranked chunk scores low, the system says it could not find the answer. Thresholds depend on the model, so calibrate them on the golden set. More options and when to skip reranking: Reranking.

"Embeddings are fast but approximate, because the question and chunk are encoded separately. A cross-encoder reads them together, so it's accurate but slow, which is why I only use it to rerank the top 20."

Saying it in an interview

"After retrieval I rerank the top 20 and keep 5, and if the best score is below a threshold I say I couldn't find it instead of guessing. I pack the chunks into a fixed budget with the best first, wrapped as tagged data with ids, so the model treats them as evidence, not instructions. The model returns an answer with citation ids. Code then checks every citation is a chunk we retrieved and the user can see, and every number appears in a source; if not, it regenerates once and then falls back. The answer ships with sources and feedback buttons, and every request is logged as one trace so bad answers can be debugged and added to the golden set."
